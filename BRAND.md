# The Marauders — identity system (working draft)

Status: **concept stage, round two.** Six directions explored, three live.

---

## Positioning

The Marauders is an **esports advisory** founded by a Valorant roster that
actually played. The audience is org owners, investors, sponsors, players and
agents — B2B wearing B2C clothes.

Two forces pull against each other, and the identity has to hold both:

1. **You are not a team.** Dressed as a roster, an advisory competes with its
   own clients and forfeits the thing it sells: outside perspective. This
   argues for restraint — no shields, no chrome, no snarling animals.
2. **You played.** That is the entire basis of the credibility. An identity so
   restrained that any management consultancy could wear it throws away the
   only proof you have.

**Round one failed test 2.** Corner brackets, a chevron and a grid were precise
and defensible and proved nothing. Round two fixes it: the player-legacy is the
*idea* in each mark, not a flavour on top.

The rule going forward: **an owner should read an instrument; a player should
read a signal only a player would catch.** Same mark, two readings. Nothing that
needs a caption.

## The name

**The Marauders.** Settled — article and plural included.

The roster name is correct precisely because the legacy is the asset. It names
the people who played. The register problem it creates ("we engaged The
Marauders" sounds like hiring a squad, not retaining advisors) is solved by the
locked descriptor, not by amputating the name.

    THE
    MARAUDERS
    ESPORTS ADVISORY

"THE" is demoted to the mono technical layer so the lockup does not sag on a
weak article, and the name stays whole. The descriptor is **not optional** — it
is the only element stating what the business is.

**Open item, blocking:** trademark search. "Marauders" is crowded across sports,
fiction and esports. Clear it before anything is printed.

### Round three — the word itself

*Marauder*, from French *maraud*, a rogue; *marauder*, to roam in search of
plunder. Irregulars. The semantics are specific and they line up with the game
almost exactly: a marauder does not hold a line, does not come through the
front, and leaves with something.

### Honest weaknesses

- **04 Crosshair** — the form is common. Its authenticity is also its risk: a
  crosshair is not inherently ownable. It gets owned through specific
  proportions plus the Signal dot used consistently, not through the shape
  alone. Strongest legibility of the three.
- **05 Thirteen** — the deepest cut and the least immediate. Nobody counts
  thirteen ticks on first sight; it rewards the second look and the explanation.
  At 16px it degrades to an arc. Acceptable, not great.
- **06 Entry** — most robust at small size (five solid squares survive
  anything). Risk: the offset square can read as a mistake rather than a move
  if the offset is ever softened. It must stay decisive.

## The heritage crest

The legacy roster mark was a Punisher-style skull.

**It cannot be carried across as-is.** The Punisher skull is a registered Marvel
trademark and Marvel has enforced it, including against small commercial users.
A roster logo is one risk profile; a firm that invoices clients, signs
sponsorship deals and puts a mark on contracts and merchandise is another
entirely. Separately, that specific skull has acquired strong political
associations over the last decade — not a legal problem, but a meaning the
brand would be carrying into rooms with investors and sponsors without having
chosen it.

**The solve is to draw our own.** `assets/crest/crest.svg` is built on the same
100-unit grid as every other mark in this system: square eye sockets, a compact
cranium, a straight four-tooth jaw, a nose. The short square teeth and the nose
are deliberate departures — the elongated fang jaw and noseless flat cranium are
what make the Marvel silhouette recognisable, and this one avoids both. It owes
that design nothing.

Still worth a clearance check before it goes on anything sold.


### What the legacy mark actually held

Seeing the original settled which parts are equity and which are exposure.

**Marvel's, structurally — cannot carry across:**

- The long teeth dropping well below the jaw, uneven, longest at the centre.
  This is *the* Punisher signature; nothing else identifies the mark as
  strongly. Keep these and nothing else you change matters.
- The domed, egg-shaped cranium and its taper.
- Slanted wedge sockets mirrored into an angry brow, with sharp inner points.
- The cheek wings flanking the teeth.

**Ours, worth keeping:**

- **The sliced wordmark** — condensed, heavy, cuts carved through the strokes.
  Not Marvel's, genuinely distinctive, and the strongest asset in the old mark.
- "THE" tucked small above the M.
- The stacked composition, wordmark over mark.
- One ink, full contrast, no gradients. The old mark was already disciplined.

### The redraw — `assets/crest/crest-b.svg`

The first attempt (`crest.svg`) was safe but swung too far: it dropped the
attitude along with the liability. `crest-b.svg` is the one to work from.

Three decisions carry it away from the Punisher while keeping the menace:

1. **Faceted cranium, not domed.** An octagonal skull with chamfered shoulders
   tapering into a straight jaw. Hard-edged and geometric, and it sits on the
   same 100-unit grid as every other mark here.
2. **Parallel sockets, not mirrored.** Both eye slots slant the *same*
   direction. Mirrored slanted eyes are the generic angry-skull move and the
   Punisher's specifically; parallel slots make the face read as glancing off
   to one side — the off-angle, the flank. This is the invention, and it is
   ownable.
3. **Five short teeth, contained in the jaw.** Even, countable, nothing
   hanging below the silhouette. Five for the roster. The teeth stop being the
   signature, which is the whole point.

The wordmark's stencil cut still needs drawing properly as custom letterforms —
what is shown is Archivo with a slice, standing in for the real thing.

### Two tiers

Keep both marks. Give them different jobs.

| Tier | Mark | Leads on |
|------|------|----------|
| One | Primary (whichever of 04–09 wins) | Deck and one-pager, contracts, invoices, proposals, sponsor and investor material, website, favicon, email signature — anything a client signs. |
| Two | Heritage crest (`crest-b.svg`) | Discord and X avatar, apparel, player-facing content, stream overlays and thumbnails — anything a player sees first. |

Together: the origin slide, once. The about page, once. Nowhere else. Two marks
competing for the same surface is how a system stops meaning anything.

The crest is **monochrome** — a crest is one ink. Signal belongs to the primary
mark. The one exception is the `crest-one-taken` variant, where a single tooth
is removed and displaced in Signal; that is the word drawn into the legacy form,
and it is an option, not the default.

## Colour

| Token | Hex | Use |
|-------|-----|-----|
| Graphite | `#14161B` | Core. All marks, all body text. Never pure black. |
| Paper | `#F7F7F4` | Ground. Never pure white for large fields. |
| Signal | `#E04E39` | Accent. One use per view. Never a fill behind body text. |
| Rule | `#D8D8D1` | Hairlines, dividers, borders. |
| Quiet | `#8A8C87` | Captions, mono labels, secondary. |

In all three round-two marks Signal marks **the decisive element** — the dot,
the thirteenth tick, the one who steps out. That consistency is what makes the
colour mean something rather than decorate.

Signal is warmer and more orange than Valorant's `#FF4655` on purpose: it keeps
competitive heat without reading as Riot merchandise.

**Alternate:** a cool analytic blue in place of Signal, pushing harder against
category convention. Worth seeing before locking.

## Type

| Role | Face | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| Wordmark & headings | **Archivo** 600/700 | Tight tracking. Institutional authority. |
| Technical layer | **JetBrains Mono** 400/500 | Uppercase, wide tracking. Labels, stats, match IDs, the "THE". |
| Body | Archivo 400 | |

The split is the strategy: the grotesk speaks to owners, the mono speaks to
players. Two families, no more.

## Usage

- **Clearspace:** one arm-length of the mark's own grid (roughly 22 of 100
  units) on all sides. Nothing enters it.
- **Minimum size:** 16px for 04 and 06. For 05, 24px — below that the tick
  count is lost and it reads as a plain arc.
- **One ink:** every mark must survive as a single flat colour. Tested for
  embroidery, etching and fax-grade print.
- **Reversed:** swap Graphite for Paper; Signal stays Signal.
- **Descriptor:** "ESPORTS ADVISORY" is locked to the wordmark in all external
  contexts. It may be dropped only where the mark appears alone as an avatar.

### Don't

- Don't outline, bevel, gradient or add a glow.
- Don't rotate the marks. Their orthogonality is the point.
- Don't spread Signal across multiple elements. One decisive element only.
- Don't soften the offset square in 06. It is a move, not a wobble.
- Don't set the wordmark in anything but Archivo.
- Don't drop the descriptor in sales, contract or sponsorship contexts.
